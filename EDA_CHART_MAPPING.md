# Matching Requirements to Chart Types: Exploratory Data Analysis with Seaborn

This document provides a systematic framework for matching analytical requirements to optimal chart types, accompanied by exploratory data analysis (EDA) implemented in [`eda_seaborn.py`](file:///d:/Akarsh/Mysore_pythonDocker/data_analysis/eda_seaborn.py) using Seaborn on the validated Zomato dataset ([`zomato_clean_validated.csv`](file:///d:/Akarsh/Mysore_pythonDocker/data_analysis/zomato_clean_validated.csv)).

## 1. Requirements-to-Chart Type Decision Framework

When conducting exploratory data analysis, selecting the right visualization depends on the **variable measurement level** (continuous vs. discrete/categorical) and the **analytical objective**:

| Analytical Objective / Question | Variable Types | Ideal Chart Type | Seaborn API | Why this Chart Type? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Univariate Distribution & Skewness**<br>*"What is the spread, central tendency, and modality of ratings and meal prices?"* | Single continuous variable (`rating`, `approx_cost_inr`) | **Histogram with KDE** | `sns.histplot(..., kde=True)` | Displays both discrete frequency binning and smoothed probability density estimates. Readily exposes skewness, bimodality, and tail outliers. |
| **2. Bivariate Continuous Relationship**<br>*"Does dining satisfaction scale linearly or non-linearly with meal cost?"* | Continuous vs. Continuous (`approx_cost_inr` vs. `rating`) | **Scatter Plot with Regression Trendline** | `sns.scatterplot()` + `sns.regplot()` | Maps point clustering, density anomalies, non-linear ceilings, and overall correlation directionality. |
| **3. Distribution Across Segments**<br>*"How do ratings vary across budget, moderate, and fine dining price tiers?"* | Categorical vs. Continuous (`price_tier` vs. `rating`) | **Box Plot with Mean Indicators** | `sns.boxplot(showmeans=True)` | Compactly summarizes 5-number summaries (minimum, Q1, median, Q3, maximum) and outlier boundaries without visual clutter. |
| **4. Group Comparison & Density**<br>*"Does online delivery adoption correlate with review volume and customer satisfaction?"* | Binary Categorical vs. Continuous (`has_online_order` vs. `votes`, `rating`) | **Violin Plot & Side-by-Side Box Plot** | `sns.violinplot()`, `sns.boxplot()` | Combines box plot metrics with rotated KDE distributions on each side, revealing multimodal engagement patterns. |
| **5. Multivariate Collinearity**<br>*"Which performance metrics and operational features are correlated?"* | Multiple Continuous Metrics | **Annotated Correlation Heatmap** | `sns.heatmap(..., annot=True, cmap='coolwarm')` | Color-codes pairwise Pearson correlation coefficients, immediately highlighting strong positive/negative associations and redundancies. |
| **6. Categorical Ranking & Metric Aggregation**<br>*"Which primary cuisines dominate and achieve the highest customer satisfaction?"* | High-cardinality Categorical vs. Continuous (`primary_cuisine` vs. `rating`) | **Horizontal Bar Plot with Sample Size Tags** | `sns.barplot(..., orient='h')` | Horizontal orientation accommodates long text labels cleanly; bar lengths provide intuitive comparison of category means. |

### Chart 1: Univariate Distributions (Ratings & Cost)
- **Artifact:** [`eda_plots/1_univariate_distributions.png`](file:///d:/Akarsh/Mysore_pythonDocker/data_analysis/eda_plots/1_univariate_distributions.png)
- **Code Pattern:**
  ```python
  fig, axes = plt.subplots(1, 2, figsize=(14, 5))
  sns.histplot(df['rating'].dropna(), kde=True, ax=axes[0], color='#2b5c8f', bins=25)
  sns.histplot(df['approx_cost_inr'].dropna(), kde=True, ax=axes[1], color='#e67e22', bins=30)
  ```
- **Analytical Finding:**
  - **Ratings:** Exhibit a near-normal, slightly left-skewed distribution centered at a median of **3.70 / 5.0**. The majority of venues fall between **3.3 and 4.1**.
  - **Meal Cost:** Severely right-skewed. While the median cost for two is **INR 400**, a long tail extends up to **INR 6,000**, confirming that budget and moderate venues dominate the volume.

### Chart 2: Meal Cost vs. Customer Rating Relationship
- **Artifact:** [`eda_plots/2_rating_vs_cost_relationship.png`](file:///d:/Akarsh/Mysore_pythonDocker/data_analysis/eda_plots/2_rating_vs_cost_relationship.png)
- **Code Pattern:**
  ```python
  sns.scatterplot(data=df, x='approx_cost_inr', y='rating', hue='has_online_order', alpha=0.6)
  sns.regplot(data=df, x='approx_cost_inr', y='rating', scatter=False, color='#c0392b')
  ```
- **Analytical Finding:**
  - Positive upward-sloping trendline ($r \approx 0.38$). Higher expenditure venues consistently hold higher rating floors (rarely falling below 3.8 once cost exceeds INR 1,500).
  - Online delivery venues (green points) cluster densely in the **INR 200 – INR 800** range.

### Chart 3: Rating Variance Across Price Tiers
- **Artifact:** [`eda_plots/3_price_tier_vs_rating_boxplot.png`](file:///d:/Akarsh/Mysore_pythonDocker/data_analysis/eda_plots/3_price_tier_vs_rating_boxplot.png)
- **Code Pattern:**
  ```python
  sns.boxplot(data=df, x='price_tier', y='rating', order=['Budget', 'Moderate', 'Premium', 'Fine Dining'], showmeans=True)
  ```
- **Analytical Finding:**
  - Median ratings rise monotonically: **Budget (3.60) $\rightarrow$ Moderate (3.60) $\rightarrow$ Premium (3.90) $\rightarrow$ Fine Dining (4.20)**.
  - The interquartile range (IQR) narrows dramatically in the Fine Dining tier, indicating consistent execution and lower variance compared to Budget venues.

### Chart 4: Channel Impact (Online Delivery vs. Dine-In Only)
- **Artifact:** [`eda_plots/4_online_order_channel_impact.png`](file:///d:/Akarsh/Mysore_pythonDocker/data_analysis/eda_plots/4_online_order_channel_impact.png)
- **Code Pattern:**
  ```python
  sns.violinplot(data=df, x='channel_label', y='log_votes', palette=['#95a5a6', '#2ecc71'])
  sns.boxplot(data=df, x='channel_label', y='rating', palette=['#95a5a6', '#2ecc71'])
  ```
- **Analytical Finding:**
  - **Review Density:** The violin plot reveals a broad upper bulge for Online Delivery venues in $\ln(1 + \text{votes})$, showing sustained multi-thousand-vote engagement, whereas Dine-in only venues taper sharply toward near-zero votes.
  - **Rating Comparison:** Online delivery venues enjoy a higher median rating (3.70 vs. 3.60) and fewer low-rating outliers.

### Chart 5: Multivariate Correlation Heatmap
- **Artifact:** [`eda_plots/5_multivariate_correlation_heatmap.png`](file:///d:/Akarsh/Mysore_pythonDocker/data_analysis/eda_plots/5_multivariate_correlation_heatmap.png)
- **Code Pattern:**
  ```python
  corr_matrix = df[['rating', 'votes', 'approx_cost_inr', 'approx_cost_per_person', 'num_cuisines', 'engagement_index']].corr()
  sns.heatmap(corr_matrix, annot=True, fmt='.2f', cmap='coolwarm', vmin=-1, vmax=1)
  ```
- **Analytical Finding:**
  - **Strongest Positive Correlation:** `votes` and `engagement_index` ($r = 0.89$), confirming that customer review volume drives social proof.
  - **Cost vs. Rating:** Moderate positive correlation ($r = 0.38$).
  - **Menu Breadth (`num_cuisines`):** Weak correlation with rating ($r = 0.12$), indicating that offering more cuisines does not guarantee higher diner satisfaction.
